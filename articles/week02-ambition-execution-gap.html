Everyone Says They're "Doing AI." Almost Nobody Is Doing It Well.

Remco Vroom · Mar 15, 2026 · 8 min read

88% of business leaders say they're using AI. Only 1% say they're doing it well. That gap is the most important number in business right now.

Welcome back to SundAI Motivation.

This week I want to talk about something almost nobody says out loud in boardrooms, but almost everyone is privately thinking: most AI initiatives are failing quietly.

Not dramatically. No press releases, no postmortems. They just don't produce anything. The pilot runs. The demo goes well. And then, six months later, it disappears from the quarterly update deck.

The Deep Dive: Why AI Projects Fail (and What the Winning 1% Do Differently)

The Simple Version

The problem isn't the AI. The tools work remarkably well. The problem is that most organizations treat AI like a feature to install rather than a capability to build.

The Context

88% of business leaders say their organization is actively using AI. But only 1% describe themselves as mature or excellent at it.

95% of AI pilots deliver zero measurable bottom-line impact. 42% of companies abandoned at least one AI initiative in 2025, up from 17% the year before. Only 6% of organizations can attribute more than 5% of their profits to AI.

The answer to why is simpler than most consulting firms would like you to believe: most organizations are optimizing for the appearance of progress rather than the architecture of results.

This is the gap between experimenting and operating.

Experimenting means: "Let's try this and see if it works." Valuable for learning. Terrible as an end state.

Operating means: "We've built this into how we work. We have someone accountable for it. We're measuring it weekly and improving it continuously."

The Analogy

Every January, gyms sign up millions of new members. Most stop coming by February. Is it a gym problem? No. The gym works perfectly. The problem is everything surrounding the gym visit: no clear program, no accountability, no tracking.

This is exactly where most companies are with AI. ChatGPT works. Claude works. But you can't just subscribe to a tool, let teams use it ad hoc, and expect transformation. You need a program, accountability, measurement, habits.

Monks Success Story

When Monks decided to go all-in on AI, we didn't start with a pilot. We started with a platform.

Monks.Flow was designed to cover the entire marketing lifecycle for clients at enterprise scale. When AI is embedded across a full workflow, outputs from one stage become inputs for the next. Results compound over time.

For Hatch, deploying Monks.Flow delivered an 80% increase in click-through rate and a 31% decrease in cost per acquisition. Those are operating numbers from a system built to run at scale.

"The gap between companies experimenting with AI and companies executing with AI is the biggest competitive divide in business today."

Your One Action This Week

Do one honest audit. List every AI initiative your organization is currently running or has run in the past 12 months. For each, ask: "Is this running in production, with measurable results, that I could describe clearly to our CFO right now?"

Circle the ones where the honest answer is yes. If you can't circle many, that's a diagnosis. Every organization that has closed the experimentation-to-operation gap started with honest assessment.

Be honest. That's the only way to start moving.
