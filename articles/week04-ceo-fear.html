74% of CEOs Are Terrified of AI. Here's What the Other 26% Figured Out.

Remco Vroom · Mar 29, 2026 · 8 min read

In the last 12 months, CEO confidence in AI dropped from 82% to 49%. That's not a blip. That's a crisis of direction, and it has a solution.

Welcome back to SundAI Motivation.

The pressure is real. The board is asking questions. Competitors are making announcements. The technology is moving faster than any implementation plan you've built. And somewhere in your organization, there are people doing things with AI that you don't fully understand yet, producing results you can't yet measure, with tools you haven't fully vetted.

But anxiety isn't a strategy. The leaders I most respect have moved through the anxiety to the other side, and they share something in common.

The Deep Dive: From AI Anxiety to AI Confidence

The Simple Version

AI anxiety in the C-suite almost always comes from the same source: pressure without direction. The leaders who've moved past the anxiety solved the direction problem first.

The Context

A year ago, 82% of CEOs expressed confidence in their company's AI strategy. Today, that number is 49%.

56% of CEOs report no meaningful financial benefit from AI investments yet. Only 14% of CFOs report measurable ROI from AI initiatives. 35% of executives admit their AI projects create an "illusion of progress."

CMOs: 72% describe themselves as overwhelmed by the pace of AI change in marketing alone. CTOs: 91% cite existing technical debt as their primary barrier to AI adoption.

The drop from 82% to 49% CEO confidence is the logical result of a lot of expectation meeting a lot of organizational friction.

The Analogy

Anxiety is almost always the result of a gap between where you are and where you think you should be, combined with the absence of a clear path between them.

The executives who've moved past AI anxiety didn't get there by consuming more information. They got there by making a real decision, with real organizational weight behind it, about where they were going and what they were willing to commit to get there. Information doesn't cure anxiety. Action does.

Monks Success Story

At Monks, we built a five-area framework: AI platform development (Monks.Flow), talent transformation (School of AI), client partnership, governance, and culture.

This produced real evidence. Adweek's first-ever AI Agency of the Year. One Show AI Pioneer Award. 92% client retention rate. Specific case results like 80% CTR improvement and 31% CPA reduction for Hatch.

Confidence in AI strategy doesn't come from having all the answers before you start. It comes from running the strategy long enough to generate the answers you need to improve it.

"The cure for AI anxiety isn't more information. It's a decision. Pick a direction. Start moving. Adjust as you go."

Your One Action This Week

Block 90 minutes with your leadership team. One question, on the table, for 90 minutes:

"If we got AI right, really right, not pilot-right but operationally right, what would our company look like in 12 months?"

Let every person answer. Write the answers down. Don't evaluate them in the moment, just collect them.

Ninety minutes. One question. Write down everything you hear. That conversation is the beginning of confidence.
