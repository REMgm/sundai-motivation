SundAI Motivation, Week 5

The Companies Getting AI Right, and the Ones Getting Left Behind

Remco Vroom · Apr 5, 2026 · 9 min read

Here is a tale of two companies. Same industry. Same moment in time. Same clients. One is growing. The other is collapsing. The difference isn't talent, technology, or budget. It's a decision that was made, or not made, a decade ago.

This week, I want to walk you through what that divergence actually looks like in numbers. The gap between the AI leaders and AI laggards has stopped being theoretical.

This Week in AI

This Week in AI This section updates with the latest AI news each week, developments translated into plain business impact for leaders.

What's Actually Happening

Publicis Groupe: +5.6% organic growth. €14.5 billion in revenue.

WPP: -8.1% revenue decline. Market cap destroyed, from approximately £25 billion down to roughly £3 billion.

Same markets. Same types of clients. The difference: Publicis started investing in AI infrastructure nearly a decade ago. They built Marcel, their internal AI platform connecting their entire global workforce, long before it was fashionable. WPP is restructuring now. Reactively. In crisis mode.

The difference in outcomes isn't subtle. It's existential.

JPMorgan has told managers to stop hiring across multiple business units. Amazon cut 30,000 corporate roles. More than 141,000 technology industry jobs have been eliminated in 2025 alone.

Wisetech Global cut 30% of its staff. Their CEO said: "The era of writing code is over."

This is a structural realignment, happening across industries, in real time.

The Analogy

In 2005, physical retailers knew e-commerce was coming. Most said: "We'll figure out online. We've got time." They didn't deny the trend. They just believed they had more runway than they did.

By 2015, Borders was gone. Circuit City was gone. Blockbuster was a cultural punchline.

The compression from "emerging trend" to "industry-defining force" took about a decade. AI is compressing that same arc from decade to years. Maybe less.

Monks Success Story

While legacy holding companies lose market value and restructure under duress, Monks moved in the opposite direction. We reduced our workforce from ~7,300 to ~6,500 and turned that into a competitive advantage — doing more with a leaner, AI-augmented team.

PUMA: A fully AI-generated brand film, produced 2.8× faster than conventional timelines. GM: Over 100 distinct assets delivered in 18 days.

Henry Cowling, our CEO: "Our point of view has always been to lean in." Not "wait and see." Lean in.

"The companies winning at AI didn't outspend their competitors. They out-decided them."

Your One Action This Week

Draw two columns. AI Leaders in your industry on the left. AI Laggards on the right.

Now ask: Which column does your company belong in today? Not which column you want to be in. Where you are, right now, honestly.

A decision. That's what separated Publicis from WPP.
