How to Prove AI ROI to Your Board, Without the Buzzwords

Remco Vroom · May 3, 2026 · 8 min read

Only 14% of CFOs say they can clearly demonstrate the business impact of their AI investments. But among those 14%? 77% report a return of 2x or more. The problem isn't that AI doesn't work. The problem is that most organizations aren't measuring it.

The Silence That's Costing You

Someone presents an "AI roadmap." Slides. Timeline. Enthusiasm. Then someone asks: "What's the return?" Silence. Or vague statements about "long-term transformation." That silence is costing your organization trust, budget, and competitive ground.

35% of CEOs admit their AI projects create an "illusion of progress" — activity that looks like transformation but isn't producing measurable outcomes. The industry calls it: AI washing.

The organizations pulling ahead did something simple: they defined success before they started.

Three metrics. That's all it takes:

1. Time saved. Hours per week returned to your team. At what fully-loaded cost per hour? Multiply across the year.

2. Quality improved. Error rates, customer satisfaction, one measurable quality signal.

3. Cost reduced. What were you spending before? What are you spending now?

Three lines on a spreadsheet, tracked every 30 days, with a 90-day target agreed up front. That's it.

The Analogy

You hire a marketing agency. At the quarterly review: "The team really learned a lot. Brand awareness is building." You'd fire them. But in AI, we accept exactly this kind of non-answer. They don't get a pass. Not when you set 90-day targets and define your metrics on day one.

AI without defined success metrics isn't a transformation. It's a budget line with a story attached.

Wesley ter Haar: "An AI story that doesn't change economics isn't anything more than a story."

"If your team says 'AI is going great' but can't show you a number, it's not going great. It's going nowhere."

Your One Action This Week

Before your next AI budget conversation, do one thing: Define exactly three metrics you will track. Set a 90-day target for each. Write them down. Specific. Measurable. Time-bound. Then hold the work to those numbers in 90 days.

That's how 14% becomes your number, and 2x becomes your floor.
